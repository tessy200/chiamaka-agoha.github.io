The Problem

Course creators and L&D managers struggle to track learner progress and prove measurable transformation. Learners lose motivation and rarely apply what they've learned. Navigate was conceived as a behavioral learning platform that bridges the gap between course completion and real-world skill application.

What I Did

Ran a complete discovery and definition cycle — from research through to a fully scoped PRD. Applied qualitative and quantitative research methods (interviews + surveys across 3 user types), mapped the competitive landscape against Coursera for Business, Coach.me, and Google Sheets-based trackers, and identified the key gap: no platform combines behavioral activation + social accountability + measurable transformation.

Designed 3 user personas (Course Creator, L&D Manager, Lifelong Learner) and built 2 journey maps including a detailed "Day in the Life" map for the course creator — surfacing 8 distinct pain points and opportunity areas. Authored the full PRD with functional requirements, acceptance criteria, NFRs, KPIs, assumptions, risks, and open questions for engineering.

Applied all 3 prioritization frameworks: MoSCoW to define MVP scope, RICE to score features by reach/impact/confidence/effort, and Kano to classify features by emotional engagement — concluding Kano was the best fit for a microlearning product where learner motivation and habit formation are the core retention drivers.

💡 PM Insight — Why Kano Won Over MoSCoW & RICE

Microlearning success depends on emotional engagement, not just functionality. MoSCoW defined scope and RICE scored impact, but Kano identified what keeps learners coming back — distinguishing between features users expect (progress tracking), features that improve satisfaction (personalised paths, offline access), and delighters (voice assistant). For a habit-forming product, that emotional mapping is the most strategic prioritization lens.

The Problem

Access to quality healthcare in Lagos is constrained by distance, cost, and availability of doctors. Patients face long wait times, fragmented records, and no reliable way to access licensed specialists remotely. HealthConnect was designed to bridge that gap — connecting patients with verified doctors anytime, anywhere through secure digital consultations, e-prescriptions, and integrated pharmacy delivery.

Product Vision

"Make quality healthcare accessible and affordable to every Lagosian by connecting patients with licensed medical professionals anytime, anywhere through secure digital consultations and integrated care services."

What I Did

Defined the product vision, set 3 strategic objectives and 10 measurable Year 1 success metrics — covering onboarding UX (under 3 mins), patient satisfaction (80%+), doctor response time (under 5 mins), regulatory compliance (100% NDPA + NITDA), and scale (200 doctors + 10,000 patients within 12 months).

Mapped 15+ stakeholders across 6 categories (internal team, leadership, external partners, end users, regulators, service providers) and placed each into a 4-quadrant influence/interest matrix. Built a detailed engagement plan — specifying communication frequency, channel, key information, and risk if mismanaged — for every high-priority group.

Surfaced a nuanced stakeholder debate: are doctors and pharmacy partners end users or service providers? Argued that both are true — they experience the platform directly and benefit from it, making their usability needs as important as patients'. This insight was used to justify dual-persona treatment in the roadmap.

Produced 3 audience-specific roadmap views from the same 5 themes, ensuring each team saw the version most relevant to their decision-making — without losing strategic coherence.

💡 PM Insight — Trade-offs & Stakeholder Conflict

Trade-off made: Prioritised appointment booking and doctor verification over wellness tips and social features. Also chose to automate initial diagnostic assistance rather than requiring direct doctor involvement — freeing doctors for complex cases while reducing response time. Top stakeholder-preferred features were pushed to future releases to protect MVP scope.

Conflict anticipated: The Founder's product vision and the Chief Medical Officer's clinical perspective are likely to diverge — especially on AI-assisted diagnosis vs. human-only consultation. Resolution approach: acknowledge both priorities, evaluate against roadmap objectives, offer alternatives, and defer contested features to a post-MVP release with supporting data from initial user behaviour.

The Problem

Gen Z users aged 18–25 are digital natives entering financial independence without the tools or habits to save. Traditional fintech is too complex, too clinical, and too slow to trust. SaveUp was designed to make saving effortless, automatic, and rewarding — with a vision to make saving a way of life, not a struggle.

What I Did

Defined the MVP scope against hard constraints: a 90-day deadline, engineering bandwidth capped at 3–4 features, a budget ceiling of 5 features, and mandatory compliance requirements. Used RICE scoring to evaluate every feature against value, stakeholder input, customer feedback, and legal needs — arriving at the 5 that would prove the core value proposition without overbuilding.

Structured a 6-sprint, 3-phase delivery plan: Phase 1 (Foundation — security + core savings logic), Phase 2 (Core Value — cash-out + analytics), Phase 3 (Launch Prep — testing, penetration test, app store submission). Each sprint had a focused goal, preventing scope drift and protecting quality.

Built a stakeholder alignment plan to secure unified approval across CEO, Engineering, Marketing, and Compliance — each with different priorities. Addressed executive pushback directly with structured rebuttals: CEO wanted uniqueness (→ Savings Interest Boost as differentiator), Engineering flagged scope (→ sprint dedicated to testing), Legal required compliance (→ Basic Security + Instant Cash-out locked as non-negotiable).

🎯 Target User

21-year-old college student or recent graduate. Digital native, variable income, overwhelmed by traditional finance tools. Values authenticity, transparency, and immediate utility.

💡 PM Insight — Handling Executive Pushback

When wearing both PM and Project Manager hats, the hardest job is defending scope under pressure. For SaveUp, the reframe was: we are trading a larger feature set for a secure, on-time launch that proves our core value proposition. Locking compliance features as non-negotiable, dedicating a full sprint to testing, and positioning the Interest Boost as the WOW differentiator meant every stakeholder got their core priority without blowing scope or the deadline.
